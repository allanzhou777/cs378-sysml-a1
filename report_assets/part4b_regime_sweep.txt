    H       N Br=Bc=8    | Br=Bc=16   | Br=Bc=32     (TFLOPs/s)
--------------------------------------------------
   32     512 |   0.0193 |   0.0350 |   0.0379
   32    1024 |   0.0198 |   0.0368 |   0.0399
  128     512 |   0.0468 |   0.0542 |   0.0463
  128    1024 |   0.0493 |   0.0576 |   0.0470

NOTE:
at H=128, Br=Bc=16 beats Br=Bc=32 -- opposite of the H=32 pattern above where 32 wins. This holds at every N tested with H=128 (512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192; see medium-regime table below). The "best Br=Bc" is NOT N-invariant once H is large enough to matter -- it depends on batch*heads (i.e. how many independent thread blocks there are relative to the GPU's 40 SMs), not just on N. See gpu_device_properties.txt occupancy cross-check: at Br=Bc=16, 4 blocks/SM are shared-mem-resident vs. only 2 at Br=Bc=32. With H=32 (32 blocks total) there's SM headroom to spare, so fewer/larger tiles (Br=Bc=32, less loop overhead) wins. With H=128 (128 blocks, several waves across 40 SMs), the extra resident blocks/SM at Br=Bc=16 apparently hide memory latency better than the loop-overhead savings from Br=Bc=32 buys back -- net win for 16.

This confirms the H=128 pattern from short/medium regimes holds at N=16384 too: Br=Bc=16 (0.0609) beats Br=Bc=32 (0.0457) at H=128, and is also *faster in wall-clock* (583s vs 776s) despite doing more tile-loop iterations per block -- the extra resident-blocks-per-SM latency hiding at Br=Bc=16 (4 blocks/SM vs 2 at Br=Bc=32, see gpu_device_properties.txt) wins outright here, not just on a flops-per-second technicality. Br=Bc=8 at H=128,N=16384 was not run (~700s+ extrapolated, low marginal value once the 8 vs 16 vs 32 trend is already clear from medium regime, where 8 never wins).

Note: Br=Bc={8,16} were NOT run at N=16384 -- extrapolating from the N=8192 wall-clock numbers above (~4x cost per doubling of N, consistent across all three regimes), Br=Bc=8 at N=16384,H=128 would take on the order of 700s and Br=Bc=16 on the order of 600s, for a single correctness/timing measurement. That extrapolated cost -- not a hard occupancy or memory limit -- is itself the empirical argument for why small Br/Bc become impractical at long context: the O(Tc*Tr) = O((N/Bc)^2) serial tile loop per block, combined with only Bc threads doing the tile-local work in parallel, means small Bc pays the full N^2 tile-count penalty without amortizing it over more per-block parallelism.
